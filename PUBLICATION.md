# Attribution, source handling, and publication

The course has two parts, each built on a different source. Both are cited in every lesson and in [References](Reference.md).

- **Chapters 1–13** are personal study notes on [The Organic Chemistry Tutor's statistics video course](https://www.video-tutor.net/statistic.html): one note per video, written in my own words, with every calculation re-checked and slips in the videos pointed out and corrected. Each note links to its original video and chapter page. The notes work through the **same example problems as the videos**. The figures, `pdf_version/` exports and `handwritten_note/` PDFs were made for these notes. The repository does not distribute the videos, video frames, transcripts, or The Organic Chemistry Tutor's own formula sheets (those in the original notes folder were deliberately not copied; [formula_sheet/00_febril](formula_sheet/00_febril) is my own).
- **Chapters 14–18** and the archived chapters in `old_chapters/` consist of original explanations, new made-up examples, new exercises and answers, and figures generated by the accompanying Python code. They follow the subject sequence of Josh Starmer's *The StatQuest Illustrated Guide to Statistics*. They do not distribute the book's pages, screenshots, cartoons, or extracted text.

Neither The Organic Chemistry Tutor nor StatQuest/Josh Starmer is affiliated with or has endorsed these notes.

The study PDFs and extracted reading files remain in the ignored `sources/` directory. The private task brief remains in the ignored `plan/` directory. Neither is required to read the notes or regenerate the figures. The `.venv/`, notebook checkpoints, and local QA artifacts are also ignored. These exclusions keep supporting local material out of ordinary additions to Git; they do not remove anything that was already tracked.

## Existing Git history needs a separate review

Older source PDFs remain in this repository's existing Git commits, even though no PDF paths were tracked in the current index at the final review. Creating new course files or ignoring `sources/` does not remove those older PDFs from existing commits, branches, tags, or a remote repository. The pre-existing removal of those files from the working tree was preserved.

Before making a private repository public, inspect both the pending changes and the history. These commands only read repository state:

```bash
git status --short
git diff --name-status
git ls-files '*.pdf'
git log --all --name-only --format=oneline -- '*.pdf'
```

If copyrighted source files are present in commits that would become public, resolve that distribution issue before publishing. Depending on the intended history, options include preparing a clean repository containing only the original course, or deliberately removing the files from the relevant history with appropriate coordination and backups. A deletion commit by itself leaves earlier versions available. No history rewrite, commit, or push was performed while preparing these notes.

## Attribution and reuse

Attribution identifies the source of ideas and directs readers to the author. It is not, by itself, permission to redistribute protected material or a guarantee of legal clearance. Fair-use and similar exceptions depend on the circumstances and jurisdiction; the [U.S. Copyright Office's guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html) explains this for U.S. law. Readers should watch the original videos on [video-tutor.net](https://www.video-tutor.net/statistic.html) and obtain the book from its [official author page](https://statquest.org/statquest-store/).

Chapters 1–13 stay closer to their source than Chapters 14–18: they follow each video lesson by lesson and reuse its example problems, although the explanations, figures and checks are my own. Keep this in mind when deciding whether to publish them and under what terms, and consider asking The Organic Chemistry Tutor for permission if in doubt.

No repository-wide reuse license has been selected on the owner's behalf. Before inviting others to reuse the course, choose an appropriate license for material you are entitled to license, distinguishing prose and figures from code if desired. Any third-party works and software retain their own terms. Making a repository visible publicly does not itself supply a reuse license for its contents.

For future contributions, write explanations in your own words, link every note to its source (video or book chapter), create or properly license figures, record sources, and keep any private reading material outside the public course. In Chapters 14–18, construct fresh examples rather than reusing the book's. Replacing names or numbers in copied passages is not an adequate substitute for original authorship.
